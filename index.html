Divergent Ankle Mechanisms Underlie Hindfoot Reversal in Therian Mammals

Duyi (Tina) Kuang & Stephanie E. Pierce · Harvard University

Do marsupials and placentals achieve hindfoot reversal through similar or different joint-level mechanisms?

X-ray reconstructions of a tree squirrel (a placental) and a Virginia opossum (a marsupial) turning the foot. Swipe through, and drag the slider on each joint to turn the feet yourself.

MethodsHow we collected the data

Tiny beads in every bone, two X-ray cameras, and the foot turned by hand (that's me). Matching the beads to CT scans rebuilds each bone's 3D motion. The opossum's right foot is mirrored to match the squirrel's left.

Hindfoot reversalWhole foot

Both feet turn almost 180°, but in opposite directions: the squirrel mostly inverts, the opossum mostly everts. Which joints make the difference?

CRJCrurotalar joint

The upper ankle. In the squirrel it is a stiff hinge. In the opossum the astragalus (yellow) swings round under the tibia, and the fibula turns with it.
